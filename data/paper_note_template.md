# English field reference

| # | Field | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One-sentence finding, in my own words | No abstract copying. If you can't write it, you didn't read it. |
| 2 | Which question it answered | Mark whether the question was genuinely open. |
| 3 | Key figure and why it is credible | Figure number + n + quantification method. |
| 4 | Method essentials | Concentrations, durations, cell lines, depth, replicates → copy into Ledger C. |
| 5 | System and limits of extrapolation | How far the claim can travel. |
| 6 | **Controls run and controls omitted** | The omission is usually your entry point. |
| 7 | **Effect size, with numbers** | A note without a number is not a note. |
| 8 | **One thing I do not believe** | Mandatory. Can't find one → read again. |
| 9 | **Hot-topic position** | Foundational / current mainstream / rising / saturating / marginal, plus who is doing it. |
| 10 | **Gaps it exposes or admits** | Copy the authors' own "remains unclear" statements; mark which one *you* could do. |
| 11 | **Where this line goes in three years** | Follow it / avoid it (crowded) / get ahead of it. |
| 12 | Interface to my project | Transferable method, reusable control value, or **competition risk** with a difference statement. |
| 13 | One executable action | "I will do Y in system X, expecting Z" → Ledger A. |
| 14 | Which of its references to queue next | PMIDs into the candidate pool. |

Fields 6, 7 and 8 are what separate a reader from a reviewer; fields 9, 10 and 11 are what you will paste into the Significance and Innovation sections of a grant. Field 12 is how the reading plan stays connected to the bench.
